AudienceTTS reads live YouTube chat aloud on your Mac and follows OBS stream state. This notice describes the data used by that workflow and the local information the app can retain.

Information accessed and why

AudienceTTS uses YouTube API Services. With your authorization, it requests the youtube.readonly permission to identify your channel, find active broadcasts and receive their live chat. The app uses this information for the chat-reading features you enable.

  • Channel and broadcast information: channel ID and title, broadcast ID and title, live-chat ID and live status.
  • Live-chat information: message text, author display name and channel identifier, message identifier, publication time, roles such as owner or moderator, and supported message types such as membership or paid messages.
  • OBS information: stream activity and reconnection state from a WebSocket connection to OBS on the same Mac.
  • Preferences and diagnostics: voice, audio output, filters, connection configuration, speech timing and operational events.

Read-only authorization does not let AudienceTTS post messages, delete messages, moderate chat or change your YouTube broadcasts. Google handles Google account sign-in in your browser; AudienceTTS does not ask for or receive your Google password.

The default settings and log location is ~/Library/Application Support/AudienceTTS/. A custom state location can change that path. Operating-system backups, copies you export and recordings you make are separate copies under your control.

Connections and sharing

The app communicates with Google for authorization and with YouTube for channel, broadcast and live-chat access. Google and YouTube process those requests under the Google Privacy Policy and applicable service terms.

OBS observation connects to 127.0.0.1, the loopback address of your Mac. AudienceTTS does not need your YouTube stream key. Speech uses installed macOS voices; this version does not send chat to a cloud speech service or upload synthesized audio.

AudienceTTS has no developer-operated service that collects your chat, and it does not sell this data, use it for advertising or train an AI model with it. If you route speech into OBS, a recording or another output, those tools may broadcast or save the spoken chat according to your configuration.

If you use the optional legacy browser mode, Google pages run in its local browser profile under Google’s own practices. This direct YouTube mode does not depend on reading your Safari tabs.

Stop, revoke access and delete local data

  1. Stop reading chat. Use Pause Speech or Stop TTS in AudienceTTS. Stop TTS suppresses reading for the current stream. Turning off Follow OBS Streams or quitting the app stops automatic chat reading. These actions do not revoke Google authorization.
  2. Revoke Google authorization. Open Google’s connected-app security settings, select the connection for your AudienceTTS OAuth application and remove its access. You can also manage connections through your Google Account. Revocation prevents further authorized API access once Google applies it.
  3. Clear local history. Use Clear Queue or stop the stream to cancel prepared speech and remove the active queue file. In AudienceTTS, open Logs → Clear Logs, then quit the app to release recent messages from memory. Files left by an app crash or forced quit are not replayed and require manual removal from the app’s own application-support folder. Revoking access does not itself erase local logs, preferences, backups or recordings.
  4. Remove saved connection credentials and preferences if needed. With the app quit, remove only AudienceTTS’s saved connection items in macOS Keychain and its own application-support data if you want a full local reset. This also removes saved setup and may include the optional legacy browser profile. Keep any settings or records you intend to retain before resetting.

This informational website cannot access or delete files or Keychain items on your Mac. Removing the app alone does not necessarily remove its application-support files or Keychain credentials. Google’s and your own recordings or backups have their own deletion controls.

When this website is served through GitHub Pages, GitHub may process normal web-request information such as IP address, user agent, request time and URL to deliver and secure the site. Questions and changes

For help or questions and complaints about AudienceTTS privacy practices, use AudienceTTS support issues. GitHub issues are public. Share only a general description; do not post passwords, tokens, OAuth client files, private chat content or unreviewed logs.

This notice should be updated when the app’s data practices change. The date at the top identifies this version. AudienceTTS is an independent app and is not endorsed by Google, YouTube or OBS. Its use of YouTube API Services is subject to the YouTube Terms of Service.
